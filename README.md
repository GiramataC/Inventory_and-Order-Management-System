*** E-commerce Database System ***

This project implements a relational database for a retail/e-commerce platform. It handles customers, product catalogs, inventory tracking, and order management with a focus on data integrity and historical accuracy.

*** 1. Database Architecture ***

The system is built on a normalized relational model consisting of five core tables:
*** - Customers: *** Stores personal profile information.
*** - Products: *** The central catalog containing current categories and pricing.
*** - Inventory: *** A 1:1 extension of the products table managing stock levels.
*** - Orders: *** Tracks transaction headers (who bought, when, and for how much).
*** - Order_Items: *** A junction table that allows multiple products per order and captures the "price at purchase" to maintain financial history.

*** 2. Key Features ***

*** Automatic Identity:*** Uses GENERATED BY DEFAULT AS IDENTITY for primary keys, ensuring the database handles unique numbering automatically.*** Data Integrity: *** Implements CHECK constraints to ensure that product prices and inventory quantities can never be negative.
*** Historical  Accuracy: *** The order_items table stores the price paid at the time of the transaction, protecting order history from future catalog price changes.
*** Referential Integrity: *** All relationships are enforced via Foreign Keys with DEFERRABLE constraints to allow for complex data operations.

*** 3. Installation & Setup ***

--> Prerequisites:
PostgreSQL 
A SQL client (pgAdmin)

--> Schema Implementation (DDL)

Run the DDL scripts to create the tables. 
Note that the order of execution is important due to table dependencies:products and customers (Parents)inventory and orders (Dependent children)order_items (Junction table)

--> Seeding Data

To populate the database with test data, use the provided seeding script. This script uses a Reset-and-Reload strategy:SQLTRUNCATE TABLE inventory, orders, products, customers RESTART IDENTITY CASCADE;
It then dynamically inserts records using JOIN logic to map customer names to their generated IDs, ensuring the script works regardless of what IDs the database assigns.

*** 4. Analytical Queries ***

The system is optimized for common retail reporting, such as:
Top Customers: Identifying high-value shoppers using SUM and GROUP BY.
Stock Alerts: Identifying products where quantity_on_hand is low.
Sales History: Reviewing historical prices paid versus current catalog prices.

*** 5. Constraints and Rules ***

Email Uniqueness: No two customers can share the same email address.
Non-Negative Values: price and quantity_on_hand must be >= 0.
Mandatory Fields: Key business data (name, email, order date) is set to NOT NULL.
